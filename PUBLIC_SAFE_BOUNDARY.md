# Public-Safe Boundary

## Public by design

- Source code, schemas, methods, and validation rules
- Synthetic and sanitized decision examples
- Known-object controls
- Public-safe normalized datasets
- Decision briefs approved for public release

## Private by default

- Sensitive raw evidence
- Exact observation locations
- Personal experience narratives
- Medical, psychological, or identifying information
- Client, government, or institutional incidents
- Credentials, keys, internal architecture, or operational procedures
- Proprietary models and confidential decision assumptions

## Decision safeguards

- Stakeholder values must remain explicit.
- Analyst-assigned scores must be disclosed.
- Scenario plausibility must not be represented as probability.
- Machine-generated candidates must not be represented as final authority.
- Minority or dissenting views must not be removed merely to create apparent consensus.
- A public release must not expose information that creates disproportionate privacy, security, or safety risk.

## Phenomena safeguards

- Observation, reported experience, candidate mechanism, and interpretation remain separate.
- Assumed range remains assumption-based.
- Generated visuals are never original evidence.
- Unresolved does not mean extraordinary.
